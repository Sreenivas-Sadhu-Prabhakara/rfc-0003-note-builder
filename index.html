RFC 0003 / Note Builder / 1969

A small thought.
A conversation started.

An unanswered question can be enough. Make a simple discussion note, give it some context, and let other people help it grow.

Early network collaborators needed a way to share ideas before they were finished. RFC 3 encouraged short notes, open questions and unpolished suggestions, and asked authors to supply a little identifying context.
This original modern adaptation helps a school club or neighborhood group put a thought into words. The examples deliberately reuse a title: a local reference can distinguish notes without forcing every title to be unique.
Model & limitations. This is a local discussion format, not an official RFC template or submission service. Filling every field does not prove quality, agreement or authority. The kind selector and unreviewed banner are our additions. Blank context is advisory; a nonempty thought enables export.
Source evidence, status and archive limitations
RFC 3, opening paragraphs (anybody may contribute); CONTENT (timely, unpolished notes and unanswered questions); FORM items 1–4 (identifier, author, affiliation, date and non-unique title).
Official status: UNKNOWN · Legacy stream. UNKNOWN is not the formal HISTORIC classification. These educational adaptations are not current Internet standards.
RFC 3 is obsoleted by RFC 10. RFC 10 is in turn listed as obsoleted by RFC 16. RFC 10 revises the note conventions and recipient list; RFC 16 adds an MIT recipient. These are historical records, not current contact or submission instructions.
The retrieved errata view lists report 8412 as Reported, not Verified. Its cited section and text do not match the cached RFC, so it is not applied. Follow-up access to the newer endpoint was unavailable; check the linked page for the latest status.
The introductory affiliations and distribution list differ. We do not resolve those differences or reuse historical contact details.

Try it with someone
A two-minute experiment
Load the minimal example. Download the one-line observation with its context still blank. Add an author and a local reference: what becomes easier for a reader to discuss? Compare the two examples with the same title.
Expected observation
A thought without punctuation still exports. The downloaded text always labels itself as a local, unreviewed discussion note.
